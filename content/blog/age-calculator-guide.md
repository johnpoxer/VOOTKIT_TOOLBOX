Choose a calendar age or an elapsed-day count according to the question you need to answer.

Use the [Age & Date Difference](/tools/everyday/age-calculator/) alongside this illustrative example.

## A practical example

Between 1 January 2026 and 1 February 2026 there is one calendar month but 31 elapsed days. Converting the month to 30 days would lose a day. For a birthday, use completed calendar years; for a project log, retain the date pair and elapsed days.

## Check the result

Check the day and month before calculating, especially when copying a date written as 03/04. Enter an unambiguous calendar date rather than assuming which country’s order the sender used.

## Try a comparison

Move only the end date forward by one day. The total-day figure should increase by one, even if the completed-year figure stays unchanged. This is a useful way to distinguish a birthday boundary from continuous elapsed time.

## What this does not establish

Age calculations do not determine legal eligibility. An organisation may apply its own reference date and rules for birthdays on leap day.
