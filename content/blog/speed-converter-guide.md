Use a familiar conversion as a check on the input unit.

Use the [Speed Converter](/tools/everyday/speed-converter/) alongside this illustrative example.

## A practical example

A speed of 36 km/h equals 10 m/s because 36 is divided by 3.6. A speed of 60 mph is approximately 96.56 km/h, not 60 km/h. The numeric value can look plausible in either unit, so copying the unit is as important as copying the number.

## Check the result

Keep the source unit with the converted result. Avoid rounding before further calculations, particularly when using speed and duration to estimate distance.

## Try a comparison

For a duration of ten seconds at 10 m/s, distance is 100 m. Repeat the check after converting 36 km/h to m/s first. Multiplying 36 by seconds without converting the hour unit is a common source of an implausible distance.

## What this does not establish

Unit conversion does not determine a safe driving speed or account for conditions. Follow local limits and use appropriate guidance for the activity.
