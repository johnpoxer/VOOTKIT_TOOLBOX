Separate the return on cash invested from the property’s operating yield.

Use the [Cash-on-Cash Return](/tools/realestate/cash-on-cash/) alongside this illustrative example.

## A practical example

If you contribute 50,000 in total and retain 3,000 annual cash flow after operating costs and debt payments, cash-on-cash return is 6%. Omitting 5,000 of closing and repair costs would shrink the denominator to 45,000 and overstate the same result as 6.67%.

## Check the result

List every initial cash payment before entering the investment total. Keep the annual cash-flow period consistent with the costs and mortgage figures used to calculate it.

## Try a comparison

Keep annual cash flow at 3,000 and change total cash invested to 60,000. The return becomes 5%. This isolates the denominator; do not attribute the change to better or worse property operations when the operating cash flow has not changed.

## What this does not establish

This ratio excludes taxes, sale proceeds and changes in property value. A positive ratio does not establish that the investment fits your risk or liquidity needs.
