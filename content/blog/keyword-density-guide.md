## Information to prepare first

Before opening the tool, collect the following:

For Keyword Density, uncertainty in **clean page copy** or **primary topic** should be visible. Record a range or source rather than quietly choosing a favourable value, so this working result cannot be mistaken for a confirmed one.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Open the [Keyword Density](/tools/seo/keyword-density/) and state the main purpose of the output before entering details.
2. Add the prepared clean page copy, primary topic, related terms details in a consistent order. Keep names, dates, currencies and units aligned.
3. Generate the first keyword density result, then compare its most important output with the original source.
4. Change **clean page copy** once to see whether the conclusion is stable or needs a second scenario.
5. Read the result from the recipient's perspective and remove placeholders, unsupported claims or fields that do not apply.
6. Save only the reviewed version together with the assumptions that explain how it was produced.

## Worked example

A draft repeats “free invoice generator” 24 times. The editor keeps it where specific, replaces redundant uses with plain references and adds missing concepts such as line items, due dates and tax.

This Keyword Density example is deliberately small enough to audit. Add real-world complexity one item at a time and preserve a baseline; otherwise a change in **clean page copy** can be confused with a change in **context around repeated phrases**.

## What can change the result

The answer is especially sensitive to **clean page copy** and **primary topic**. Test both before relying on it. The final input—**context around repeated phrases**—may look secondary, but it can change how another person interprets the output even if the headline figure or layout stays the same.

## How to interpret the output

There is no universal ideal density. Search systems evaluate meaning, usefulness and many signals; the count is a diagnostic, not a ranking target.

Read this keyword density output as evidence for the decision to find accidental repetition and missing vocabulary while preserving natural language. Check whether it answers that purpose, whether its important statements trace to a source, and whether a reviewer can see what is included and excluded without a separate explanation.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Chasing an exact percentage.** Correct it before the output becomes a shared record.
- **Counting navigation as article copy.** Correct it before the output becomes a shared record.
- **Replacing every synonym.** Correct it before the output becomes a shared record.
- **Adding keywords after the answer is already complete.** Correct it before the output becomes a shared record.

With Keyword Density, clean formatting improves comprehension but does not validate **clean page copy** or the final conclusion. Resolve the listed mistakes before this output becomes a client document, published page or permanent record.

## Quality-control checklist

- Verify the Keyword Density result independently against its most important source.
- Confirm **clean page copy**, **primary topic**, dates, currencies and units.
- Remove example text and every field that does not apply to this workflow.
- Check the exported result on a phone as well as a desktop.
- Test each link, QR code or call to action in the final version.
- Keep a dated source copy so later edits cannot silently replace the approved result.
