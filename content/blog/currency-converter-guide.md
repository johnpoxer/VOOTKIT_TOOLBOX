A currency converter answers a clean mathematical question: what is one amount worth at a stated exchange rate? A real card purchase or international transfer adds a second question: what rate and fees will the provider actually apply? Those results are often different.

## Use the quoted rate as a reference

Open the [Currency Converter](/tools/finance/currency-converter/), select the source and destination currencies, enter the amount and check the rate timestamp. Vootkit obtains reference exchange-rate data over the network, so this is one finance tool that requires an internet connection.

The basic conversion is:

**Converted amount = source amount × applicable exchange rate**

Rates may be quoted in the opposite direction. A EUR/USD quote is not interchangeable with USD/EUR; one is the reciprocal of the other.

## Mid-market rate versus customer rate

The mid-market rate is a reference between market buy and sell prices. A bank, card network, cash bureau or transfer service may add a spread by offering a less favourable customer rate. It may also charge a fixed fee, percentage fee or receiving fee.

To compare providers, calculate how much the recipient actually receives for the same total amount paid. A service advertising “zero fee” can still earn money through its exchange-rate spread.

## Timing changes the result

Foreign-exchange markets move. A card transaction may be authorised on one day and converted when it settles later. Weekend markups can apply when markets are closed. Cash rates at an airport may differ materially from online reference rates.

For budgeting, use a modest buffer rather than planning to the last cent. For invoices, state the billing currency and who bears conversion or intermediary-bank fees. The [Invoice Generator](/tools/business/invoice-generator/) can help create a clear document, but it does not set the exchange rule for a contract.

## Avoid dynamic currency conversion surprises

At some foreign merchants or cash machines, you may be offered a charge in your home currency. This is called dynamic currency conversion. The displayed convenience may include a provider-selected rate. Compare it with the likely card-network conversion and fees before choosing; do not assume home-currency billing is cheaper.

## Common conversion mistakes

- Using today's rate for a historical transaction.
- Ignoring fixed transfer and receiving fees.
- Comparing the sent amount instead of the received amount.
- Reversing the currency pair.
- Treating a reference rate as a guaranteed executable rate.
- Forgetting card foreign-transaction fees or weekend markups.

## Questions people ask

### Why is my bank's result different?

The bank may use a different timestamp, customer rate, spread and fee schedule. Check the transaction disclosure.

### Are rates truly live?

Reference feeds update on their own schedule and are not guaranteed executable quotes. The page should show when data was obtained.

### Can I use today's rate for taxes?

Tax authorities may prescribe a transaction-date rate, official annual average or another method. Follow the rule in your jurisdiction.

### Does converting twice lose money?

It can. Each conversion may introduce another spread or fee, even when the reference arithmetic appears reversible.

The converter is an informational estimate. Confirm the final rate and all fees with the financial provider before transferring or spending money.
