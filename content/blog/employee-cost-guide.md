Build an employment budget from separately stated cost assumptions.

Use the [True Employee Cost](/tools/tax/employee-cost/) alongside this illustrative example.

## A practical example

For a hypothetical salary of 40,000, employer tax at 10% adds 4,000 and pension at 5% adds 2,000. Benefits of 1,000, with equipment and overhead set to zero, bring the model total to 47,000, a 17.5% uplift over salary. These rates illustrate the arithmetic; they are not the rates for a particular country.

## Check the result

Compare the salary-only figure with the total, then list recruitment, equipment and office costs outside the model. One-time expenses should not silently become recurring annual benefits.

## Try a comparison

Add a hypothetical 2,000 equipment budget to the earlier example. Total model cost becomes 49,000. Decide whether that amount recurs annually or belongs only in the first-year budget; the input does not make that accounting decision for you.

## What this does not establish

Employer contributions can have thresholds and caps that a flat-rate estimate misses. Verify local rules and employment terms before using the figure as a hiring commitment.
