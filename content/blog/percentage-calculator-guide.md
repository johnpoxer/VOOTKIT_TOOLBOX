Distinguish a percentage of a number from a percentage change.

Use the [Percentage Calculator](/tools/everyday/percentage-calculator/) alongside this illustrative example.

## A practical example

A rise from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase because the increase of 20 is divided by the starting value of 80. Returning from 100 to 80 is a 20% decrease. The same two values therefore produce different percentages depending on the direction.

## Check the result

Write the starting value first and name the quantity being compared. For a discount, compare the reduction with the original price, not the final price. Check that a zero starting value does not produce a meaningful percentage-change claim.

## Try a comparison

Compare a 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease starting from 100. The sequence gives 120 and then 96, not 100. The second percentage uses a different base, which also explains why percentage changes cannot simply be added and cancelled.

## What this does not establish

Percentage points are different again: a rate rising from 10% to 12% increases by two percentage points, or 20% relative to the original rate.
