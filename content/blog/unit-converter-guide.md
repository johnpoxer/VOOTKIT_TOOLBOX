## What the Unit Converter does

Converts within Length, Mass, Area, Volume, Speed, Data and Time, plus Temperature, which is handled separately because it does not scale from zero.

Everything except temperature is a simple ratio against a base unit, which is why the conversions are exact rather than rounded lookups.

## Practical advice

The volume units are US customary. A US gallon is about 3.785 litres and an imperial gallon is about 4.546, a difference of 20% — which is enough to ruin a recipe or badly mislead a fuel-economy comparison. Check which one your source meant before trusting the number.

## Common questions

### Is 1 KB 1000 or 1024 bytes?

Here it is 1024, the binary convention operating systems use. Storage manufacturers use 1000, which is why a "500 GB" drive shows as roughly 465 GB in your file manager. Neither is wrong; they are different definitions, and the gap grows with size.

### Why is temperature separate?

Because it has no true zero to scale from. Every other conversion is multiplication by a ratio, but Celsius to Fahrenheit needs an offset as well as a factor — 0°C is 32°F, not 0°F, so a simple ratio gives nonsense.

### Are these US or imperial gallons?

US. The two differ by about 20%, along with pints and quarts. If your source is British, the numbers will not match, and on fuel economy the error is large enough to matter.

### How precise are the results?

The factors are the standard exact definitions — an inch is exactly 0.0254 m, a mile exactly 1609.344 m. Precision is limited by ordinary floating-point arithmetic, which is far beyond anything you would notice.
