Understand the simplified US-style self-employment component separately from income tax.

Use the [Self-Employment Tax](/tools/tax/self-employment-tax/) alongside this illustrative example.

## A practical example

For hypothetical revenue of 20,000 and expenses of 5,000, model profit is 15,000. Applying the model’s 92.35% base adjustment gives 13,852.50; at 15.3%, the self-employment component is approximately 2,119.43. Income tax is a separate calculation and is not included in that component.

## Check the result

Do not treat this as a universal tax formula. The actual US calculation can involve the Social Security wage cap, wages from employment and Additional Medicare Tax; the model does not fully apply those rules.

## Try a comparison

Change only the illustrative expenses from 5,000 to 6,000. Model profit falls to 14,000, and the simplified SE component becomes approximately 1,978.14. Only allowable expenses belong in an actual tax calculation; the numerical effect does not establish deductibility.

## What this does not establish

Confirm the filing year, deductible expenses and current official instructions. Use a proper return or qualified tax professional for the amount due.

## Official reference

[IRS explanation of self-employment tax](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes). Confirm the tax year and use Schedule SE for an actual return.
