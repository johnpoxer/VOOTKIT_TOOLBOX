Compare loan payments while distinguishing principal and interest from total housing costs.

Use the [Mortgage Calculator](/tools/realestate/mortgage-calculator/) alongside this illustrative example.

## A practical example

A zero-interest loan of 120,000 over ten years would repay 1,000 per month. Adding interest raises that payment; adding property tax or insurance raises household cost further, even when the principal-and-interest calculation is unchanged. This simple baseline helps reveal a misplaced term or extra zero.

## Check the result

Compare two offers using the same balance and term, then record fees and any changing-rate period separately. Read the repayment total as well as the initial monthly figure.

## Try a comparison

Shorten a model’s term while keeping the principal and annual rate fixed. The monthly payment normally rises, while total interest can fall. Compare both effects with your own cash-flow constraints rather than using a smaller interest total as an automatic instruction.

## What this does not establish

The result is a model, not a lender’s offer. It does not establish eligibility or account for every charge, repayment condition or future rate change.
