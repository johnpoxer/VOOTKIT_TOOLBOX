Reconcile a pay estimate with its deduction bases and pay frequency.

Use the [Take-Home Pay](/tools/tax/paycheck-calculator/) alongside this illustrative example.

## A practical example

With hypothetical gross pay of 40,000, a 5% pension contribution is 2,000. In this model, a 10% income-tax rate then applies to 38,000, giving 3,800; a 5% social rate applies to gross, giving 2,000. With no other deductions, annual net is 32,200, or about 2,683.33 across twelve payments.

## Check the result

Check the payslip’s actual deduction bases and the number of payments per year. Twenty-six fortnightly payments and twenty-four twice-monthly payments produce different per-payment amounts.

## Try a comparison

With the same 32,200 annual model net, dividing across twenty-six fortnightly payments gives about 1,238.46 each. The annual figure has not changed. A smaller payment amount can reflect a different schedule rather than a lower salary.

## What this does not establish

Rates and deduction treatment vary by jurisdiction and pension scheme. This model does not replace payroll software or an official withholding calculation.
