Convert a meeting’s full date and time rather than relying on a remembered offset.

Use the [Time Zone Converter](/tools/everyday/timezone-converter/) alongside this illustrative example.

## A practical example

For a meeting with colleagues in London and New York, enter the actual meeting date and the named source zone. Repeat for a date near a daylight-saving change: the two cities do not necessarily change clocks on the same day, so a familiar five-hour difference may not apply.

## Check the result

Record the converted calendar date as well as the time, especially when a meeting crosses midnight. Use named city zones instead of ambiguous abbreviations such as CST.

## Try a comparison

Make a second conversion for the following week using the same local meeting time. Around a clock change, another city’s time may shift even though the organiser’s wall-clock time stays fixed. Include the date in reminders to avoid a stale offset.

## What this does not establish

Rules depend on the timezone data available to the browser. Recheck important appointments against the calendar invitation and keep the device software current.
