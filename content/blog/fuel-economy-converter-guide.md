Fuel economy is quoted four different ways depending on where the car was sold, and two of them run in opposite directions. Comparing a US review against a European one without converting produces confident nonsense.

## What the Fuel Economy Converter does

Converts between **MPG (US)**, **MPG (UK)**, **L/100km** and **km/L**.

**US and UK MPG are not the same figure**, because the gallons differ: 30 MPG US is about 36 MPG UK for exactly the same car. That gap alone explains many baffling car-forum arguments.

Open the [Fuel Economy Converter](/tools/travel/fuel-economy-converter/) and follow the settings and checks below.

## The four units

| Setting | What it means |
|---|---|
| MPG (US) | Miles per US gallon — 3.785 L |
| MPG (UK) | Miles per UK gallon — 4.546 L |
| **Same car** | **30 MPG US ≈ 36 MPG UK** |
| L/100km | Litres per 100 km — **lower is better** |
| km/L | Kilometres per litre — higher is better |
| MPG and km/L | Distance per fuel — higher is better |
| L/100km | Fuel per distance — inverted |
| Conversion | Not linear between the two conventions |

## How to use it

1. Enter the figure and say which unit it is in.
2. Read every other unit.
3. Check which MPG a source means before comparing — it often does not say.

## Practical advice

When a car review quotes MPG, find out whose gallon it means. A US publication quoting 30 MPG and a UK one quoting 36 MPG may be describing the identical car, and someone comparing the two numbers concludes the second is 20% more efficient. This is the commonest error in cross-border car comparisons.

## Common questions

### Why are US and UK MPG different?

Because the gallon is. A US gallon is 3.785 litres, a UK gallon 4.546 — about 20% larger — so the same car travels further on one UK gallon. Same efficiency, different number.

### Why is L/100km inverted?

It measures fuel consumed per fixed distance rather than distance per fixed fuel, so a smaller figure means less fuel. It is arguably the more useful convention for budgeting, since fuel is what you buy.

### Is the conversion a simple multiplication?

Between MPG and km/L, yes. Between either of those and L/100km, no — it is a reciprocal relationship, which is why you cannot convert by scaling and why halving one does not halve the other.

### Which unit should I use?

Whichever your region uses, so your figures match the prices and reviews around you. When comparing across regions, convert first — every time.
