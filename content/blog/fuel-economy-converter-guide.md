Identify which gallon a quoted MPG figure uses before comparing vehicles.

Use the [Fuel Economy Converter](/tools/travel/fuel-economy-converter/) alongside this illustrative example.

## A practical example

A vehicle consuming 8 L/100 km travels 12.5 km on a litre. That corresponds to about 29.4 MPG with US gallons and 35.3 MPG with imperial gallons. The vehicle has not become more efficient; the gallon has changed.

## Check the result

Write the gallon convention beside every MPG figure. For L/100 km, a smaller number means lower fuel use; for km/L or MPG, a larger number means more distance from the same fuel.

## Try a comparison

Compare 8 and 10 L/100 km directly. The latter uses two additional litres per hundred kilometres, or 25% more fuel over the same distance. MPG moves in the opposite direction because its distance-per-fuel relationship is reciprocal.

## What this does not establish

Conversion standardises units, not test conditions. City and highway figures, driving style and load can still make two published numbers unsuitable for direct comparison.
