Understand the assumptions behind a liability estimate before comparing it with a policy.

Use the [Auto Cover Estimator](/tools/insurance/auto-insurance-estimator/) alongside this illustrative example.

## A practical example

With assets of 100,000 and annual income of 40,000, the current model adds two years of income to assets, producing 180,000. That is a formula output, not an insurer’s quote, a legal minimum or a recommendation that 180,000 of cover is appropriate.

## Check the result

Compare the model with the policy’s per-person, per-accident and property-damage limits separately. A single total cannot establish how much a particular claim would pay.

## Try a comparison

Keep income unchanged and increase entered assets by 20,000. The model exposure rises by 20,000 because it is an additive estimate. A real policy premium will not necessarily move in the same proportion; that requires an insurer’s quotation.

## What this does not establish

Premium and deductible inputs provide context; this model does not price a new policy or predict a claim. Ask the insurer about exclusions and local limits.
