Sign PDF helps you place a signature mark where requested while preserving a reviewable copy. The fastest workflow is not the one with the fewest clicks; it is the one that preserves the right source, uses settings chosen for the destination and includes a deliberate check of the export.

## What the Sign PDF does

Draw a signature and stamp it onto a PDF page. Open the [Sign PDF](/tools/pdf/pdf-signature/) to follow the workflow in this guide.

Sign PDF performs this file operation in your browser after its required code has loaded. Vootkit does not need to upload the source for this operation, but the exported document still deserves the same privacy care as the original.

## Decide what the finished file must do

Before using Sign PDF, identify the destination: email, upload portal, website, print, archive, application or internal review. That choice controls format, dimensions, quality and what must remain editable or searchable. A technically successful sign pdf operation can still produce the wrong result for its destination.

For Sign PDF, prepare:

- **Final PDF.** Use the reviewed source rather than a forwarded or compressed copy.
- **Signature image or drawing.** Confirm this against the destination requirement before processing.
- **Signer authority.** Record the choice so the result can be reproduced later.
- **Page and position.** Record the choice so the result can be reproduced later.
- **Date.** Record the choice so the result can be reproduced later.

## Choose settings from the destination backward

Start with **date**, because it defines what the recipient or platform will accept. Then set **signature image or drawing** only as high as that job needs. Maximum quality, resolution or page size is not automatically safer: it can make the output slower, harder to send and no more useful at its real display size.

For Sign PDF, make a second test version when you are unsure. Change one setting only, compare both outputs at the size the recipient will use, and keep the smaller or simpler version only when meaningful detail and required behaviour remain intact. This one-variable comparison is more dependable than changing format, quality and dimensions together.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Preserve the original and work from a clearly named copy.
2. Open [Sign PDF](/tools/pdf/pdf-signature/) and add the intended source file.
3. Set **signature image or drawing** for the actual destination rather than choosing the maximum automatically.
4. Process one representative result first when the source contains mixed pages or a batch of different images.
5. Inspect the areas most likely to fail: small text, faces, signatures, transparent edges, page order or fine lines.
6. Export with a descriptive filename and reopen it outside the tool before sending or replacing anything.

## Worked example

A freelancer reviews the final agreement, places a signature on the stated line, adds the date and retains the unsigned and signed versions separately.

This scenario tests Sign PDF against a concrete requirement. If **final PDF** or **signature image or drawing** changes, keep the first result as a baseline so you can identify which choice affected quality, size or usability.

## What changes—and what does not

A visible signature image is not automatically a cryptographic digital signature. Legal acceptance and identity requirements vary by transaction and country.

The Sign PDF result should be judged by fitness for purpose. Compare the output with the source at normal viewing size and at 100% zoom, then test any behaviour the destination needs: text selection, transparency, links, form fields, print margins or platform acceptance.

## Common mistakes

- **Signing a draft.** This usually creates the largest avoidable failure.
- **Placing the mark over text.** Check this in the first test export.
- **Sharing a reusable signature image.** Add it to the final review rather than assuming the tool can infer it.
- **Claiming cryptographic verification.** Add it to the final review rather than assuming the tool can infer it.

## Quality and privacy checklist

- Keep the original document until the recipient or destination accepts the new version.
- Verify **final PDF** and **date** against the job requirement.
- Reopen the exported file and confirm its type, dimensions or page count.
- Inspect content that carries meaning: names, totals, signatures, labels, faces and fine edges.
- Remove private pages, metadata or visual details only with a method that truly removes them.
- Use a new filename so the source and output cannot be confused.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Sign PDF upload my file?

The Sign PDF operation runs in the browser on your device. The page itself and its processing libraries must load, but Vootkit does not need to upload the source file to perform this operation.

### Should I delete the original after the export works?

Not immediately. Keep the original until the Sign PDF output has been reopened, checked and accepted by its destination. This operation and any later conversion, cropping, compression, redaction or page edit can discard information that cannot be recreated.

### Why can the output look different from the source?

Format capabilities, fonts, colour handling, transparency, compression, rendering scale and page geometry can all affect appearance. For Sign PDF, check **signature image or drawing** and the limitation explained above first.
