Compare property operations separately from the way a purchase is financed.

Use the [Cap Rate Calculator](/tools/realestate/cap-rate/) alongside this illustrative example.

## A practical example

An illustrative property valued at 200,000 receives 18,000 annual rent and has 6,000 annual operating expenses. Net operating income is 12,000, giving a 6% cap rate. A mortgage payment changes the investor’s cash flow, but does not belong in this unlevered operating comparison.

## Check the result

Reconcile the expense total with management, insurance, maintenance and vacancy assumptions. Reducing expenses simply to obtain a better percentage makes the comparison less useful.

## Try a comparison

Keep the property value at 200,000 and increase operating expenses by 2,000. NOI falls from 12,000 to 10,000 and cap rate from 6% to 5%. The example shows why comparable expense assumptions matter as much as advertised rent.

## What this does not establish

The percentage is not a forecast of sale value or a complete return. Compare properties using the same expense definitions and obtain local professional advice before investing.
