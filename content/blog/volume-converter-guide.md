A recipe calling for a gallon means two different amounts depending on which side of the Atlantic wrote it — and the gap is 20%, which is more than enough to ruin the recipe or the fuel-economy figure.

## Practical advice

A UK pint is 568 ml and a US pint is 473 ml — a difference of nearly 20%. In a recipe that is the gap between working and not; in a fuel-economy figure it makes a car look dramatically more or less efficient than it is. If a source does not say which system it uses, the spelling usually tells you: "litre" and "colour" mean UK.

## Common questions

### Why are US and UK gallons different?

They descend from different historical standards and were never reconciled. The US kept an older wine gallon; the UK redefined its gallon in 1824 as the volume of ten pounds of water. The 20% gap has persisted ever since.

### Is a cup here US or UK?

US, at about 237 ml. A metric cup is 250 ml and the UK does not really use cups at all, preferring weight. For baking especially, weigh ingredients if you can — it removes the ambiguity entirely.

### How much is a UK pint?

About 568 ml, against 473 ml for a US pint. It is why a pint of beer is noticeably larger in Britain, and why converting drinks measures across the Atlantic needs care.

### Which units here are metric?

Litres, millilitres and cubic metres. Everything else is US customary apart from the UK gallon, which is listed separately precisely because the ambiguity causes errors.
