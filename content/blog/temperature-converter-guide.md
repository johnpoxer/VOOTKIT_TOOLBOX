Distinguish an absolute temperature from a change in temperature.

Use the [Temperature Converter](/tools/everyday/temperature-converter/) alongside this illustrative example.

## A practical example

A temperature of 20 °C is 68 °F because the conversion includes an offset. An increase of 20 Celsius degrees is an increase of 36 Fahrenheit degrees, not 68. For a recipe’s oven setting, convert the absolute temperature; for a laboratory temperature difference, convert the interval.

## Check the result

Check the scale written on the source. A quick familiar reference is that 0 °C corresponds to 32 °F; this helps identify a swapped input scale.

## Try a comparison

Convert −40 °C to Fahrenheit as a cross-check: both scales read −40 at that point. For other temperatures, avoid converting with a ratio alone because their zero points differ. Kelvin also uses an offset relative to Celsius.

## What this does not establish

Conversion does not account for oven calibration, pressure or measurement uncertainty. Use validated instructions for safety-critical temperature decisions.
