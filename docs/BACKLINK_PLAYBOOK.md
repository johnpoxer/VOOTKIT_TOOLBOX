# Vootkit Backlink Playbook

Everything needed to submit Vootkit to directories and pitch roundups, in a form you can copy and paste. Written 31 July 2026.

**Set expectations first.** Backlinks are the slowest of the three SEO levers. Directory listings get indexed in days to weeks; ranking movement from them takes months. Nothing here is a quick win — it's groundwork that compounds. If you want something that moves a number this week, the Cloudflare cache rule is a better use of an hour.

**What not to do, ever.** Don't buy links, don't use PBNs, don't pay for "500 backlinks for $20", don't mass-submit to directories nobody reads. With a domain this young those are how you get manually penalised, and recovery takes longer than the ranking would have. Everything below is a real listing on a site with real users.

## 2. Where to submit — priority order

Do them in this order. The first four are worth more than the rest combined.

### Tier 1 — do these first

**AlternativeTo** — alternativeto.net
The strongest single listing available to you. Vootkit is a genuine free alternative to Smallpdf, iLovePDF and TinyPNG, which is exactly what the site exists to catalogue. List against each of those three separately. High-authority domain, real users, sends actual referral traffic.

**Product Hunt** — producthunt.com
One shot, so don't waste it. Launch Tuesday–Thursday, 00:01 PT. Lead with the privacy angle rather than the tool count — "261 tools" sounds like a link farm, "your files never leave your device" is a position. Have the maker comment written in advance.

**SaaSHub** — saashub.com
Same alternative-to model as AlternativeTo, less traffic, still a clean followed link and quick to submit.

**Slant** — slant.co
Answer "What are the best free PDF tools?" style questions. Community-voted, so a genuinely useful answer outranks a promotional one.

### Tier 2 — worth an hour

- **Awesome lists on GitHub** — search `awesome privacy`, `awesome self-hosted`, `awesome productivity`. Submit a PR adding Vootkit with one accurate line. Maintainers reject anything that reads like marketing.
- **Hacker News** — Show HN. Only if you can take blunt feedback; the audience is technical and will poke at the WebAssembly claims. Genuinely privacy-preserving tools do well there. One shot, like Product Hunt.
- **Indie Hackers** — a build log post, not an ad.
- **Reddit** — r/privacy, r/software, r/webdev, r/DataHoarder. **Read each subreddit's self-promotion rules first.** The pattern that works: answer someone's actual problem, mention the tool as the solution. The pattern that gets you banned: posting a link.

### Tier 3 — background

- Tool aggregators and "free tools" directories — low individual value, but cheap.
- Your own profiles: GitHub, LinkedIn, X, Crunchbase. Mostly nofollow, but they establish the entity and Google uses them for brand association.

## 3. The roundup pitch

Higher effort, higher value. Search for pages already ranking for `best free pdf tools 2026`, `best online image compressor` and similar, then email whoever wrote them.

Hit rate is low — perhaps 1 in 20. The ones that land are worth far more than any directory.

```
Subject: Vootkit for your [free PDF tools] roundup

Hi [name],

I read your piece on [exact title] — the point about [specific thing they actually
said] matched my experience.

I built Vootkit (vootkit.com), a set of free PDF, image and video tools. The
difference from most of the ones on your list: the processing runs in the browser
via WebAssembly, so files are never uploaded. No queue, no daily cap on processing,
nothing stored server-side.

Might be worth a line if you update the post. Happy to answer anything, and equally
happy if it's not a fit.

[your name]
```

Rules: personalise the first line with something specific from their article or don't send it. One follow-up after a week, then stop.

## 4. Assets that earn links on their own

Directories are a floor. Links that arrive without asking come from things people want to reference.

**The strongest candidate you already have:** a public, accurate table of **file size limits** — Discord 10/50/500 MB, Gmail 25 MB, WhatsApp 100 MB documents / 16 MB video, and the common government portal caps. That information is scattered and often out of date across the web. Keep one page correct and current and people will cite it, because there's nowhere better to point.

Second: **honest benchmarks.** "We compressed the same 50 photos with five tools — here are the sizes and the artefacts." Specific, checkable, and the kind of thing other writers link to rather than redo.

Both are worth more than fifty directory listings, and both play to what you already have.

---

## 5. Tracking

Check monthly, not weekly — nothing moves faster than that.

- **Google Search Console → Links** — your own data, free, authoritative for what Google has actually seen.
- **Ahrefs Webmaster Tools** — free for verified sites, more detail than GSC.

Record what you submitted and when. Most listings take 2–6 weeks to appear as a link in GSC, and it's easy to forget what's outstanding.

---

## 6. Realistic expectations

| Timeframe | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Week 1–2 | Listings live, first referral clicks |
| Month 1–2 | Links start appearing in Search Console |
| Month 3–6 | Any ranking effect, if it comes |

Ten to twenty good listings is a reasonable target for a young site. Beyond that the returns fall off sharply, and effort is better spent on the link-magnet assets in §4.

**One honest caveat.** With ~400 real visitors a week, a directory listing may send more traffic than it sends authority. That's fine — early referral traffic is useful in its own right. Just don't mistake a spike in visits for a ranking improvement.
